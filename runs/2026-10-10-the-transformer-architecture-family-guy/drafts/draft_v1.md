# The Answer Box

"Stop eating. Everybody stop eating, right now!"

"What is wrong with you, Peter?"

"I bought a box, and I want you all to see it!"

"That is a beige box, Peter, and I can see it from here."

"I bought a beige box off a guy in a truck, and the thing finishes your sentences for you."

"Sentences."

"Any sentence you want. You start it, it finishes it, and it knows the word."

"You bought a beige box off a truck guy, and now you're telling me it knows words."

"I'm telling you, it knows my word, and I'm gonna prove it to you tonight!"

"It knows the word. You start a sentence, and it knows where you're going with it."

"Peter, a box does not know anything."

"This one does. It knows. It knows. It knows."

"Are you kidding me? I am looking right at it, and it's beige."

(laughs) "So let's you and me bet. The good chair says it cannot finish one sentence."

(sighs) "Peter, you cannot bet the good chair, and I mean it."

"Here's the deal: if it cannot finish one sentence, then you keep the chair, and I shut up about it forever."

"And if it can?"

"Then I keep my chair, and I never shut up about it, ever."

"Deal! Everybody heard that, so it counts!"

"Deal."

"And this is a beige affront to God, and I want no part of it."

"Stewie, it's a box, and you're a baby."

"It is a beige box with opinions, and I shan't be mocked by a box."

"Okay, one word, everybody, and watch closely: one word from the start steers the whole sentence."

"That is not how anything works, and you know it."

"Lois, watch!"

"Do you know what I think, Lois? I think you're all just jealous of my box."

"I'm gonna prove it to you, and then you're gonna feel really silly."

"You're all gonna watch, and you're all gonna lose, and you know it."

"Where did you even get a box, Peter?"

"From a guy in a truck, and you and me are gonna settle this tonight."

"Can I just say one thing, please?"

"Shut up, Meg!"

* * *

"Next!"

"Long one coming down the line, so slice it."

"Unbeliev..."

"...able."

"Two tickets, and one word, so keep it moving."

"Ticket four four seven one."

"Ticket four four seven two."

"One rule: one card per ticket, and one ticket each."

"Pull the cards for me."

"Pulled, and it's numbers."

"So it's just numbers?"

"Numbers only, sir, and I mean that completely."

"What does the card say the word means?"

"This card doesn't say anything. It's numbers."

(deadpan) "Same ticket, same card, and that's the whole rule."

"Now hold the card up against the ticket it came from."

"I can't read the ticket."

"You can't read it at all?"

"I can't read anything, sir. Same ticket, same card."

"Then how do you know you pulled the right one?"

"I don't know that. Same ticket, same card."

(pause) "Next!"

"Wait! Hold on! Look at the tray!"

"What about the tray?"

"No tags on any of these, and no positions either."

"So what?"

"Swap these two tickets, and watch what the tray does."

"Swapped, and the tray didn't move."

"Same reading, so swap them back."

"Same reading again, and nothing changed at all."

"Nothing changes. Now the sticker machine, one and two."

"Stickers on."

"And now swap those same two tickets for me."

"Reading flipped."

"Flipped how?"

"Whole thing means something else now."

"So the stickers matter."

"Stickers matter, and the cards don't."

"And the cards are the meaning, right?"

"Cards are numbers, sir. Meaning is not in the building."

"Then where does the word come from?"

"It comes out the far end, and we don't ask questions. Next!"

"Someone upstairs keeps asking for a long one."

"Tell them I'm still slicing for them."

"But that's the whole word."

"There is no whole word. Next!"

"That's it? That's the whole sentence?"

(deadpan) "That's the sentence. Next!"

* * *

"Unbeliev. It said unbeliev, so I won, and you all saw it!"

"It said a broken word, Peter."

"Unbeliev is most of a word, and most of a word is a word."

"It is not a word. It is a fragment."

"It looked up the word, Brian. It found my word, and it gave me most of it."

"It didn't look up anything."

"Brian, come on."

"It drew a piece, Peter. Same ticket, same card. There's no word for it to look up."

"How does it know my word, then?"

"You saw it, Brian. You saw the whole word."

(sighs) "Fine! Then you win round one, and my remote's in now."

"You're giving up your remote."

"Watch this. This time the first word steers the whole thing, and you're all gonna see it."

"Oh, here we go."

"And everybody watch the first word, because the first word is the whole sentence."

* * *

"Wanted cards on the left, and offering cards on the right. No talking, please."

"I want you to read the rule off the card, like a professional."

(reads) "One rule in this building: every wanted card against every offering card. No exceptions."

"Every single card?"

"Every card. Every card. Every card."

"Score them all for me."

"Scored. Two, one, and five."

"Divide every score by root sixty four."

"Stamped, and every last one."

"Now the row has to add up to one hundred percent."

"And it adds up to one hundred, so we're fine."

"Then pour the drink in those exact percentages, and one hundred percent of it."

"Blended. Sixty him, twenty five her, fifteen the other one."

"Whose drink is that one?"

"That's his now, so he's part other people."

"Neat."

"Hold on. A word changed upstairs."

"Which word changed?"

"First one, way up at the start."

"Then score everything again, and see what you get."

"Scored again."

"Everything is different now."

"Every percentage on every card, all at once."

"All of them?"

"All of them. All of them. All of them."

"And the first word never even met these cards."

"That first word meets every card. That's the whole job."

"So somebody decided the first word matters."

"Nobody decided anything. A card on the left talked to a card on the right, so that's your score, and that's your drink."

"Just tell us who matters, and we can all go home."

"Everyone matters at a different percentage. Next!"

"And where did the last list go, the one from before?"

"There is no last list. There's the score right now, and it's already different."

"That sounds exhausting, honestly."

"Extremely. Next!"

* * *

"See that? I changed one word, and it changed its whole answer."

"Nobody's deciding, Peter."

"Lois, it knows where I'm going."

"Nobody's deciding. It added up percentages, and everything changes when you change the start. You watched it happen."

"So it's paying attention, like a person pays attention to you."

"And the dishes are in now, so the dishes are on the table."

(sighs) "You cannot bet the dishes, and you know you cannot."

"Then the dishes are in, and round two is mine."

"You are totally losing badly, Peter, and I'm embarrassed for you."

"You saw what it did, and I saw what it did, and we both know what that means."

"Give me my remote, and I'll show you what my box can do."

"Do you know what I'm gonna do to that box when I win?"

"Ask it something harder, then. Tell it to think harder."

"It doesn't think."

(to the box) "Think harder, box! Think!"

* * *

"Document in, and it's going to floor one."

"Toss the whole pile together, so every page touches every page."

"Tossed, sir, just like I showed you."

"Now I want you to fix your page, and only your page. Don't touch your neighbor's page."

"I'm not touching his page."

"And don't read his page either."

"I can't read, sir. I fix the page I'm holding."

"You'll fix your page, and you'll pass it up to me."

"And I want you to do it with a smile, for me."

"Somebody tried to file my page in the cabinet downstairs."

"There is no cabinet."

"Where do I file it, then?"

"You don't file it. You fix your page, and you pass it up."

"But last week's pages, they have to be somewhere."

"There is no last week. There's this document, and that's all there is."

"Okay. Pour the original back on top, then."

"All of it? On top of the new page?"

"On top. It goes on top of the change."

"So we keep the original."

"We add the original. Nobody is keeping anything here."

"Now smooth the binder, and take the lumps out."

"Smoothed, and I did it myself."

"Floor two. Toss the pile again."

"Again?"

"Every floor, and every page, every time."

"How many floors are there?"

"Floors. Floors. Floors."

"Six, and then it's finished."

"Is it filed?"

"It's more finished."

"Not more filed?"

"More finished, not more filed. Toss the pile."

"I keep waiting for somebody to write a recipe."

"Nobody writes a recipe. Toss the pile."

* * *

"It wrote 'the boat.' We weren't even talking about a boat."

"See? It's boring."

"It's wrong, and it's boring."

"So it kept our old sentences, from before, from the other dinners, way upstairs."

"Nothing's filed, Chris."

"Brian, let the boy talk."

"Nothing's filed. It's remixed on every floor, and nothing upstairs keeps a sentence."

"Then why is it so boring?"

"Because boring is honestly common, Chris. That's a different thing entirely."

"Can you all just watch me be creative for one minute?"

"You heard me, Chris. I want my answer, and I want it now."

"Okay. New plan, so listen to me: I make it creative."

"Peter, don't."

"Where's the dial?"

"There is no dial."

"There's a dial, and it says creativity, and I can read."

"Peter, do not turn the..."

"Too late!"

* * *

"Bars up!"

"One bar for every snack in the building."

"Ninety two snacks, so ninety two bars."

"He doesn't know what the man wants."

"Nobody knows what the man wants. Roll the die."

"Weighted by the bars, every single time."

(flat) "Rolled, and it came up mat."

"Drop it."

(flat) "Dropped into the tray."

"Now ask again, with mat in the tray."

"With mat in the tray, new bars, and new chances."

"It asked itself again."

"It always asks again. One snack at a time, every single time."

"Hold on. He turned the dial."

"Which dial did he turn?"

"That one says creativity, right on the front."

"Chances are flattening quickly."

"Those bars are all about the same height now."

"Not the same, but flatter. And the pickled egg is climbing."

"How much is it climbing?"

"Pickled egg had a sliver. Now it has a slice."

"I want one thing from you: a truer answer."

"He turned it up for truer."

"Give me a better one, and I'll turn it up for you."

"I want the answer I already have in my head."

"It's more creative."

"It's more random. Pickled egg. Pickled egg. Pickled egg."

"It keeps saying pickled egg."

"It keeps saying pickled egg, so that's what a flat chart does. It's not truer, it's stranger."

"Okay, new strategy: I'll tell it the whole plan first, and then it can..."

"Mat."

"I wasn't finished talking."

"Pickled egg."

"I'm still telling it the plan, and then it..."

"Then it picks, and then it asks again. It doesn't wait for the plan, sir."

"And the little light came on. It knows it messed up."

"There's no little light. That's a sticker."

"Turn the dial up higher, then."

(flat) "Dial at maximum. Chances are flat."

* * *

"That's an egg. That's a whole jar of eggs."

"And the remote's gone. Gone. I don't have the remote anymore."

"I told you."

"It's not smarter, Peter. It's stranger."

"Lois, it's totally creative now."

"It's not smarter. It's stranger. You turned up noise, and noise is loud."

"And it planned the whole thing anyway. It knew what I wanted from the very start."

"It doesn't plan. It picks one, and then it asks again."

"It's just being coy with me, honestly."

"Do you know how much I paid for this thing?"

"It doesn't plan. It picks one. Then it asks again."

"You saw the eggs, you saw the light, you saw everything, and I'm the one who paid for it."

"Fine! New rule! Do it one at a time! One at a time!"

"Peter, that's not how..."

"One at a time!"

* * *

"Eight of you, same cards, and go."

"Same cards, same instant, and eight rankings going up."

"I rank him first."

"I rank her first."

"I rank the other one first."

"That's eight different lists, all at once."

"All at the same time?"

"All at the same time, and nobody waits for anybody."

"I want you to work at the same time, all eight of you, for me."

"Staple them side by side, and then merge them once."

"Stapled, side by side, and merged into one."

(deadpan) "One list out of eight."

"Side by side, merged, and nobody went first."

"Now do it again, one at a time."

"Sir..."

(screaming) "One at a time! Line up!"

"And they're lining up."

"But the line is seriously not moving."

"First one won't go until the second one's finished."

"Second won't go until the third one's finished."

"So the queue's jammed."

"Stalled."

"Timeout."

"Ding."

"Do it again, right now."

"Can't. It's stuck."

"Unstick it."

"We could unstick it, or you could let them work at the same time."

"One at a time!"

(muttering) "Then it stays stuck."

* * *

"It's stuck. It's been stuck for a while now."

"Timeout, and that's a forfeit."

"Peter. The chair."

"Fine. Meg gets the chair. Meg!"

"A chair? Like, the good chair, the one by the TV?"

"Meg gets the good chair, everybody."

"I can't believe you're giving my chair to Meg."

"I've wanted that chair since I was little."

"Not now, Meg."

"But listen: one more sentence, and if it finishes it, I take the chair back."

"Peter, you already lost."

"Can I have one more, Lois? One more, and then my chair comes home."

"One more, all by itself. Watch it finish the whole thing, all by itself."

* * *

"Bars up, and quiet this time."

"One bar for every snack in the building."

"He's not shouting at it now, so watch it work."

"Roll the die."

"Weighted. Dropped."

"Now ask again, with the pick in the tray."

"New bars."

"Ask again."

"Again."

"Again."

"One at a time. One snack at a time, and that's the whole trick."

"And back down the slicer line it goes."

"Ticket four four seven one."

"Same ticket, same card."

"Numbers only. It never had the word. It only ever had numbers."

"Last pick."

"Dropped."

"And the sentence is finished."

"Say it, Peter. Say the whole thing."

"The boat..."

"...is..."

"...unbelievable."

"It finished it. It finally finished the whole entire sentence."

"You watched it work, and you saw it ask again, and you know what that means."

"You and me both watched it, and I know what I saw."

"By picking one at a time, and asking again."

"So it knew."

"It drew the likely one, and it got lucky. That's all that happened."

"Ding."

"It's asking us again."

"Asking who?"

(flat) "It asks again into nothing. It always asks again."

* * *

(laughs) "I won! I won the bet, and nobody says otherwise!"

(quietly) "He did finish the sentence, Peter."

"And the good chair's mine again, forever."

"Peter..."

"It gets us, Lois. All this time I thought it was a box. It gets us, you know?"

(pause) "Peter."

"We're a family, and it gets us."

"It obviously understands the family. It obviously understands us."

"Ding."

"Barely."

"What?"

"It said 'barely.'"

"That's not..."

"Can I just say something, please?"

"Not now, Meg."

"Ding."
